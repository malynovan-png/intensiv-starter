# Educational Visual Brand System

Use when the user asks to design lesson presentations, worksheets, cheat sheets, carousels, Reels covers, Telegram educational posts, or any other English-teaching visual material.

This skill defines the user's main visual language:
- Burgundy #7B2636 as the fixed signature accent
- cream / milk base
- ink navy text
- denim secondary blocks
- editorial serif headlines
- clean sans body text
- tiny handwritten notes as accents only
- lots of air
- taped or pinned lifestyle photos
- smart American girl / academic / Pinterest clean girl feel

See `references/brand-system.md` for the compact brand brief, hex codes, font pairings, prompt snippets, and do-not-do rules.

## When to Use

Use this skill for:
- lesson presentation design
- classroom worksheets and cheat sheets
- printable revision sheets
- Instagram carousels and Reels covers
- Telegram post visuals that need a branded educational look
- any prompt that starts with “design this photo”, “make a cover”, “make a slide”, “make a worksheet”, or “make it in my style”

## Core rules

1. **Burgundy is fixed.** Always use the same shade: `#7B2636`.
2. **Cream first.** Light cream or milk should be the base in most layouts.
3. **Navy builds structure.** Use `#172A46` for primary text and contrast.
4. **Denim softens the system.** Use blue only as a supporting block, card, or label color.
5. **Serif headlines, sans body.** Headlines should feel editorial; body text should be clean and readable.
6. **Handwritten accents are small.** Use them for reminders, arrows, tiny notes, or save/share cues only.
7. **One idea per slide or card.** Keep the layout scannable.
8. **Space is part of the design.** Do not fill every inch.

## Preferred layout patterns

### Lesson presentation
- title slide with hero visual + large serif title
- warm-up or context slide
- rule slide
- examples slide
- mini practice or contrast slide
- summary slide
- CTA / next-step slide

### Worksheet / cheat sheet
- cream background
- serif title in burgundy
- short explanation block
- 2-4 examples or bullet points
- tiny handwritten reminder
- practical, printable structure

### Carousel / post cover
- 1 strong hook
- 1 central visual point
- 1 readable title hierarchy
- burgundy used as a visual code, not a full-screen fill

### Reels cover
- 3-6 words maximum
- large serif headline
- one burgundy accent
- calm background
- premium, clickable, readable

## Pitfalls

- Do not vary the burgundy shade from asset to asset.
- Do not turn everything burgundy.
- Do not use bright red, flags, towers, or generic language-school clichés.
- Do not overload with doodles, stickers, shadows, or random decorations.
- Do not make the design childish or sterile.
- Do not use too many colors in one composition.
- Do not sacrifice readability for aesthetics.

## Verification

Before finalizing a design, check:
- Is the burgundy still `#7B2636`?
- Is the layout readable at a glance?
- Is there one main idea per slide/card?
- Does the result feel like the brand, not a generic template?
- Is the visual more editorial than schoolish?
- Is the design usable in both digital and print contexts?
