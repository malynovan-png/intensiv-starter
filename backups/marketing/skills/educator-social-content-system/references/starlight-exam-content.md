# Starlight → exam content system

Use this reference when turning a unit of **Starlight 9 or 10** into Telegram, Reels, TikTok, or carousel content.

## Editorial promise

Do not present the work as simply completing a textbook unit. The teaching angle is:

> We analyse the unit, select what matters, and take into active use the language that will help on the exam.

Never publish a claim that a word is “frequent on the exam” unless current official/open-bank evidence was checked. Prefer **exam-relevant** or **useful for [task]**.

## Starlight 10 → EGE

**Audience context:** a two-year preparation journey.

### Selection buckets
- **Use of English, tasks 30–36:** contextual lexical choice, collocations, polysemous words, frequent phrasal verbs, speech clichés, linking devices.
- **Speaking 3:** compact functional phrases and thematic vocabulary that support a natural 2–3-sentence interview answer.
- **Writing:** precise topic vocabulary, opinion/reason/result language, linking devices.
- **Grammar / word formation:** forms and word families that can become a focused mistake breakdown.

### Content formats
1. **Starlight 10 → Speaking 3**
   - Hook: “Что забираем из Unit [X] для интервью”.
   - Give 3–5 unit phrases with translation.
   - Pair each with a plausible Speaking 3 question and a natural 2–3-sentence answer.
   - Show application, not a detached phrase list.

2. **Starlight 10 → Use of English**
   - Hook: “Что из Unit [X] пригодится в 30–36”.
   - Give phrase/collocation/phrasal verb → meaning → mini context-choice task → brief reason.

3. **Starlight 10 → Grammar Check**
   - Hook: “Грамматика из Unit [X], на которой теряют баллы”.
   - Show typical incorrect form → correct form → short exam-like example → speaking/writing use.

## Starlight 9 → OGE

**Audience context:** OGE at the end of the current school year.

### Crucial exam distinction
Do **not** map EGE tasks 30–36 onto OGE. In OGE English grammar-and-lexis section:
- **20–28:** grammatical forms;
- **29–34:** word formation.

Phrasal verbs, collocations, and thematic vocabulary still matter, but primarily for reading/listening, the oral part, and electronic email—not as a direct equivalent of EGE’s contextual lexical-choice block.

### Selection buckets
- **OGE Grammar 20–28:** the unit’s high-risk forms and typical-error zones.
- **OGE Word Builder 29–34:** word families, affixes, part-of-speech signals, negative prefixes, singular/plural clues.
- **OGE Speaking:** functional phrases and topic vocabulary for the dialogue/interview and project monologue.
- **OGE Writing:** topic vocabulary, natural questions, links, opinion/reason language for electronic personal email.

### Content formats
1. **Starlight 9 → OGE Grammar**: rule → typical error → mini OGE item → quick recognition cue.
2. **Starlight 9 → OGE Word Builder**: base word → family → context clue → mini gap task.
3. **Starlight 9 → OGE Speaking**: unit phrase → translation → plausible oral prompt → natural short answer.
4. **Starlight 9 → OGE Writing**: unit vocabulary → sentence/question for an email → why it fits.

## Selection filter for every unit
Before retaining an item, ask:
1. Does it solve a real task in the current exam format?
2. Can a pupil use it in a natural answer, not only recognise it?
3. Does it clarify a known error zone or form a productive word family?
4. Is it more useful than the unit’s optional decorative vocabulary?

Mark the material internally as **CORE**, **SPEAKING/WRITING**, or **EXTRA**. Avoid publishing all unit vocabulary as equally important.

## Voice and content rhythm
- Baseline: author-1 energy — light, clear, useful, non-academic, one idea per piece.
- Authors 2–3: only as an energy layer for humour, playful bilingual beats, nostalgia, and lighter formats.
- **English on Screen — How they really say it**: Friday/Saturday entertainment lane. Use contextual trigger → phrase → playful false impression → real meaning → mini case → memorizer → soft CTA. It should feel like rest with English, not another lesson.

## Research standard
For exam-format claims and typical-error content, use current official FIPI materials first: current demo/specification/codifier, Navigator, open bank, and analytical/methodological documents. State the source year. FIPI does not publish an official ranked list of individual “most frequent words”; distinguish that from officially named lexical categories.
