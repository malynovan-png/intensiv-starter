# Role-based social content

Use this skill when the user asks for Telegram or Instagram content planning, post ideas, captions, series, hooks, or rewrites that should center on her real work and identity.

## When to use
- The user asks for a Telegram post, Instagram caption, carousel, reel script, or weekly content plan.
- The user wants content to sound personal, lived-in, and useful rather than generic.
- The user wants posts about her main roles: English Teacher, curator of «Интеллект», and tutor.
- The user wants a recurring content structure or a reusable content system.
- The user asks for a post about an event, lesson, observation, or real working day.

## Content priorities
1. Lead with the user’s first three roles: English Teacher, curator of «Интеллект», tutor.
2. Use personal experience as the main source of content.
3. Support the roles with family or daily-life details only when they help the post.
4. Avoid abstract audience-first framing unless the user explicitly wants it.
5. Keep the writing warm, practical, and natural.

## Default workflow
1. Identify the platform: Telegram, Instagram, or both.
2. Identify the content class: lesson post, event post, life post, series post, reel, carousel, or weekly plan.
3. Pick the role that should lead the piece.
4. Use the matching structure from `references/content-formats.md`.
5. Keep the post concise unless the user asks for a longer version.
6. Return ready-to-post text first.

## Style rules
- Mirror the user’s mixed Russian-English voice when she mixes languages.
- Keep the tone warm, clear, and human.
- Prefer concrete scenes, examples, and named roles over broad abstractions.
- Do not over-polish the text into a press release.
- Do not add extra commentary before the draft unless the user asked for it.
- Use short paragraphs and clear breaks when the rhythm matters.
- Make the post feel like it came from a real working week, not a template.

## Preferred structures
### 1. Role-based Telegram posts
- Hook about a real problem or real situation.
- Short context from the user’s work.
- 3–4 practical points or a clear mini-story.
- A reflective closing.
- A light CTA only if it fits.

### 2. Teaching and grammar posts
- Strong title or hook.
- Short introduction.
- Main rules in blocks with examples.
- Short summary or cheat sheet at the end.
- Optional question or CTA.

### 3. Event-based posts
- Short intro with place, time, or situation.
- Concrete details from the event.
- A brief reaction or meaning.
- A closing line that lands emotionally.

### 4. English Life posts
- Real-life situation first.
- Useful phrases with translation.
- Optional UK/US note.
- One bonus term or idiom.
- Short ending question.

### 5. Phrases from series posts
- One phrase that looks simple but has a twist.
- Clear meaning in Russian.
- Short example or dialogue.
- Memory hook.
- CTA at the end.

## Output expectations
- For a single post, show the finished draft first.
- For a content plan, give the plan in day-by-day format with the leading role named.
- For a series, keep the structure consistent across entries.
- If the user gives a sample and says it is the preferred style, treat that sample as the current target.

## Pitfalls
- Making the post too generic or audience-abstract.
- Forgetting that the user wants content mostly about her first three roles.
- Overloading the post with explanation when the user asked for a ready draft.
- Using the wrong structure for the content class.
- Turning a personal post into a neutral brand caption.

## Verification
- The draft reflects the intended role and content class.
- The structure matches the chosen format.
- The voice feels natural in the user’s mixed-language style.
- The final text is ready to post without extra rewriting.
